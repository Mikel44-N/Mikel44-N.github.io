FEATURED DATA ANALYTICS PROJECT

Analyzing U.S. Electric Vehicle Market Share

An end-to-end analysis of U.S. vehicle registration data examining EV adoption, fuel-market composition, state-level differences, and policy implications.

Project Overview

Audience

U.S. Government Transportation Board

Data

Vehicle registration data covering 50 states and Washington, D.C.

Tools

Excel, MySQL / SQL, and Power BI

Business Context

The purpose of this project was to analyze the U.S. vehicle market and provide insights that could support future transportation planning, particularly around electric vehicle adoption and charging infrastructure.

The analysis focuses on differences in vehicle composition across states and examines where EV adoption is relatively higher or lower.

Key Questions

1. What percentage of vehicles in each state are EVs, PHEVs, HEVs, and gasoline?

2. Which states have the highest EV adoption rates and which states lag?

3. Which alternative fuels are significant compared with niche fuel categories?

4. Where should policymakers investigate EV infrastructure investment based on adoption patterns and gaps in support?

5. How does California compare with other large states such as Texas, Florida, and New York?

Power BI Dashboard

The dashboard summarizes the U.S. vehicle market, EV adoption, alternative-fuel composition, and comparisons between major states.



Key Findings

  • Gasoline remains the dominant vehicle fuel category, with approximately 242.87 million vehicles compared with approximately 3.56 million EVs.
  • EV adoption varies substantially across states, ranging from approximately 3.41% in California to approximately 0.13% in North Dakota.
  • Vehicle counts and adoption rates provide different perspectives. California has the largest EV count and also has the highest EV adoption rate among the states analyzed.
  • Ethanol/Flex (E85) is the largest alternative-fuel category, with approximately 20.24 million vehicles.
  • California has approximately 1.26 million EVs and an EV adoption rate of 3.41%, compared with Florida at 1.37%, New York at 1.16%, and Texas at 0.89%.

Recommendations

  • Develop a phased, long-term EV transition strategy with measurable adoption targets and periodic assessments.
  • Use state-level EV adoption rates to identify locations requiring further investigation and targeted support.
  • Investigate the factors limiting EV adoption in low-adoption states before designing targeted interventions.
  • Study California's policies, infrastructure, incentives, and market conditions to identify approaches that may be adaptable to other states.
  • Consider existing alternative-fuel adoption patterns as part of a broader transportation transition strategy.

Tools & Workflow

The project followed an end-to-end analytics workflow from data preparation through analysis and visualization.

Excel SQL MySQL Power BI Data Cleaning Data Validation Data Modeling Data Visualization

Limitations & Data Gaps

This analysis is primarily based on vehicle-registration data. It identifies differences in EV adoption and vehicle market composition, but it does not establish the causes behind those differences.

The dataset also does not contain charging-infrastructure data, incentives, vehicle affordability, consumer behavior, or other factors that would be needed to determine specific infrastructure requirements.

Additional data in these areas would strengthen future analysis and allow more specific infrastructure planning.
